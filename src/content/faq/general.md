description: "Practical answers about using GarageAI and offering your GPU."

faqs:
  - question: "What is GarageAI?"
    answer: "A two-sided marketplace for local AI inference. Garage owners (operators) connect a GPU they already own and offer open models on it. Buyers call those models through one OpenAI-compatible API at https://api.garageai.eu/v1, or use the chat in the portal, and pay per token. The operator earns the token price for the requests their garage serves."
  - question: "What hardware do I need to offer my GPU?"
    answer: "A machine that can run an open model with acceptable speed: typically a Mac with Apple silicon or a Linux machine with a capable GPU. There is no fixed minimum spec. Every model has to pass an automated acceptance test through the gateway, which measures time-to-first-token and tokens per second, before it can be sold."
  - question: "Which operating systems and runtimes are supported?"
    answer: "macOS, Linux and Windows. Supported runtimes are Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp, vLLM, SGLang and Paddock (beta). Unsloth, MLX on Apple Silicon and Lemonade on AMD also work. You keep your own OS and runtime; have it running with a model loaded, or let garageai runtime suggest Xyntetik Runner and install it for you, then one line installs GarageAI Bridge, which opens its encrypted tunnel and registers your models."
  - question: "How long does it take to connect a garage?"
    answer: "A few minutes once your runtime is running a model. Sign in at app.garageai.eu/connect and run one line: in the terminal on macOS and Linux, curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/magnusfroste/garageai/main/cli/install.sh | sh -s -- connect, or in PowerShell on Windows, irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/magnusfroste/garageai/main/cli/install.ps1 | iex; garageai connect. Neither contains anything secret. It installs GarageAI Bridge, which looks at your GPU, memory, runtime and models and opens the browser with a code; you check that it is your computer, pick a garage name and approve with one click. Bridge then opens its encrypted tunnel to the gateway and registers your models, the portal runs an acceptance test with a real request, and the page shows the steps live until \"Your garage is live\". It asks for your password once. You can also run the line first and create your account on the page it opens. If something stops, the page shows what is wrong and how to fix it, and garageai doctor shows the same on the machine. Bridge is new: report problems on GitHub or to powerup@garageai.eu."
  - question: "What is GarageAI Bridge?"
    answer: "Bridge is how a garage connects to GarageAI: one small open-source program, garageai, on macOS, Linux and Windows. garageai connect links the machine to your garage and opens Bridge's own encrypted WireGuard tunnel to the gateway (no VPN app needed, and it runs next to Tailscale), a heartbeat service keeps your offered models and their context windows in sync every 5 minutes, garageai doctor shows what runs on your machine, what is wrong and how to fix it, and garageai uninstall removes it again. Bridge never reports prompts, keys or command lines. If you use an AI agent such as Claude Code, garageai mcp gives it Bridge's read-only checks and troubleshooting guide. Bridge updates itself to the current stable release, and garageai pause takes your garage off the market for a while. GarageAI Smart can download models in demand that fit your machine (Ollama). Next, it will pause automatically when you game or render; later, a control panel will show what your GPU can earn and take you live with one click. Install and connect on macOS and Linux: curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/magnusfroste/garageai/main/cli/install.sh | sh -s -- connect; on Windows, in PowerShell: irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/magnusfroste/garageai/main/cli/install.ps1 | iex; garageai connect"
  - question: "Is Windows supported?"
    answer: "Yes, since Bridge 1.0 (October 2026). Sign in at app.garageai.eu, open Connect your GPU and pick the Windows tab: paste the line into PowerShell. Bridge asks Windows for Administrator once (it installs two services), you approve your PC in the browser, and Bridge opens its encrypted tunnel to the gateway: no VPN app, no firewall rule, and your runtime (Ollama or LM Studio) stays on localhost. The tunnel runs as a Windows service and the heartbeat as a scheduled task, so the garage comes back by itself after a restart; Bridge updates itself. Ollama runs in your user session, so keep the PC logged in (a locked screen is fine). On a laptop, garageai doctor tells you how to keep it awake on the power adapter. NVIDIA cards are used by the runtime; AMD and Intel graphics built into the processor run on the CPU, which is fine for small models. vLLM and SGLang on Windows (through WSL2) will follow later."
  - question: "Is it safe to share from my computer?"
    answer: "Yes, with three things you should know. Your computer opens no ports and accepts no connections: Bridge opens one outgoing encrypted tunnel to the GarageAI gateway, and the only thing that comes back through it is inference requests for the model you offer, to your runtime on localhost. Nothing else on your computer or your home network can be reached. What you should know: you trust GarageAI with the requests (whoever controls the gateway can use your GPU, not your files), Bridge updates itself from our releases (signed releases are on our list; you can turn self-update off), and your runtime is a program we did not write (keep it updated). The whole answer, and how to check it yourself, is on the Security page: garageai.eu/security."
  - question: "Do I need to open ports on my router?"
    answer: "No. GarageAI Bridge dials out to the GarageAI gateway over an encrypted WireGuard tunnel, so it works behind a home router, through NAT and on a changing address. You open no ports, garages cannot reach each other, and only the gateway can reach your runtime. If your network blocks outgoing UDP, Bridge can use GarageAI's self-hosted NetBird mesh instead (garageai connect --tunnel netbird)."
  - question: "What can GarageAI reach on my machine?"
    answer: "One thing: the port of your inference runtime, through the encrypted tunnel your machine opened. GarageAI cannot log in to your machine, read your files, reach other ports or reach other devices on your home network, and other garages cannot reach you at all. Buyers never see which garage served them or its address. GarageAI Bridge is open source. It carries the tunnel itself, with no VPN app or network interface, and forwards only your runtime's port to the runtime on localhost, so your runtime can keep listening on 127.0.0.1 and stays closed to your home network; Bridge also passes only inference requests to it, so nothing can download or delete models through the tunnel. It also installs a small heartbeat that reports which models you serve. (If you use the NetBird fallback instead, the runtime has to listen on all addresses, which also opens it to your home network; restrict the port with your firewall on a shared network.) You can check everything with garageai doctor and leave with garageai uninstall. The full explanation is in 'Security for garage owners' in the GitHub repository (docs/garage-security.md)."
  - question: "Is my data safe?"
    answer: "Traffic is encrypted in transit: TLS from you to our gateway on an EU VPS (Hetzner, Helsinki), and WireGuard from the gateway to the garage. The gateway does not store your prompts or responses. The prompt itself is processed in plain text on the operator's machine. Operators agree not to log, store or read prompts or responses; a garage that does is removed and its account closed. Operators are not yet formally verified, so this rests on the terms, not on technology. Verified garages and data-processing agreements are planned before we sell to buyers with sensitive data. Until then, don't send sensitive or personal data through the marketplace."
  - question: "May I look at the prompts my garage processes?"
    answer: "No. Buyers' prompts and the model's responses are confidential. The operator terms forbid logging, storing, reading, forwarding or analysing them; a garage found doing so is removed from the marketplace and the account is closed. By default none of the supported runtimes write prompts to disk, so just don't enable request logging on a garage (vLLM --enable-log-requests, SGLang --log-requests, llama.cpp --verbose). If you need it to debug your own setup, pause the garage first."
  - question: "How do operators get paid?"
    answer: "You set your own price per million tokens, input and output, when you create your garage. You earn that price for every token your garage serves, whether the buyer picked your garage or the request came through the pool. There is no platform fee during launch (a platform fee will come later). Earnings are tracked per token. The wallet and payouts are being built and are coming next."
  - question: "What does it cost to use?"
    answer: "Each garage sets its own price per million tokens, input and output, and you pay per token at the price of the garage that served your request. Cached input, a prompt prefix the garage has already processed, is billed at its lower cache-read price. You use prepaid credits, topped up by card via Stripe, with no subscription. The model catalogue in the portal shows the prices for each model."
  - question: "Who sets the prices?"
    answer: "The garages do. GarageAI has no price list of its own: each operator sets a price per million tokens for the models they offer. There is no separate pool price; a pool request is billed at the price of the garage that answered it."
  - question: "What is the difference between Pool and Specific garage?"
    answer: "Specific garage sends your requests to a named garage you choose, for example for location, and you pay the price it has set. Pool uses one model name across every garage offering that model: each request goes to one healthy garage, is billed at that garage's price, and is retried on another garage if it fails. Both are shared capacity, not exclusive; booked or reserved capacity is planned."
  - question: "How does the pool choose a garage?"
    answer: "Only garages whose model has passed the acceptance test, and keeps passing regular checks, take part. Requests are spread across those healthy garages in proportion to a weight, so every healthy garage gets traffic, not only the fastest. Weights based on each garage's price are being rolled out, so a good price will earn a larger share. If a garage fails, the request is retried on another one."
  - question: "Which models are available?"
    answer: "Open models that operators choose to serve and that pass the acceptance test. The current list is in the model catalogue in the portal at app.garageai.eu, and it changes as garages come and go."
  - question: "How is GarageAI different from other decentralised GPU networks?"
    answer: "Residential GPUs selling inference is not new in itself: there are crypto-based inference networks, hourly GPU rental marketplaces and API aggregators. What we have not seen elsewhere is the combination: named garages you can pick plus a shared pool, local GPUs and verified datacenter providers in the same catalogue and API, payment in euros by card or invoice instead of tokens, our own encrypted tunnel to an EU gateway with no inbound ports, and onboarding that gets a garage live in minutes with one line and a click in the browser. If you know a European service that already does all of that, we would like to hear about it."
  - question: "Is it OpenAI-compatible?"
    answer: "Yes. The API at https://api.garageai.eu/v1 is OpenAI-compatible, so the OpenAI SDKs, agents and coding tools work by setting the base URL and your GarageAI API key."
  - question: "Can I be both a buyer and an operator?"
    answer: "Yes. One account can buy inference and offer GPUs, and you can register several garages under the same account."
  - question: "Is GarageAI open source?"
    answer: "The building blocks are open source (WireGuard, LiteLLM and NetBird), and GarageAI's gateway setup (infra/gateway/README.md) and GarageAI Bridge (cli/), the program that connects a garage, are in the public GitHub repository under the MIT license."
  - question: "Can my company run private garages?"
    answer: "Yes, talk to us. Companies can use the API, offer their own GPUs on the marketplace, or run private garages. Contact powerup@garageai.eu about a pilot."
  - question: "Where do solar panels and EV batteries come in?"
    answer: "That is the long-term vision, not today's product. The idea is that garage nodes can eventually run largely on locally produced energy (rooftop solar, home batteries and EV batteries). Today any machine on the grid can join."
